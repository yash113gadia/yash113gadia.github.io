The situation

The work is solid. The shopfront isn't.

Property managers and realtors don't pick a maintenance vendor on a whim — they vet you online first, then hand you keys to other people's buildings. That decision is made on your website in about ten seconds.

Right now that website is a DIY GoDaddy build. It works, but it signals "small and informal" to exactly the B2B clients who sign the biggest, most repeatable contracts. And your real advantage — a system to submit maintenance requests — is hidden instead of being the loud, obvious reason to choose you.

What's holding you back

Five fixable gaps — each one costing you contracts.

Issue 01

The site looks self-built, so you look small

A DIY GoDaddy template reads "weekend handyman," not "vendor I'd trust with twelve units." For B2B clients, that perception decides who gets the contract.

→ Bigger accounts go elsewhere
Issue 02

Your best feature is three clicks deep

The maintenance-request flow should be a one-tap "Submit a Work Order" on every page. Buried in a menu, it might as well not exist.

→ Easy-to-hire advantage, wasted
Issue 03

Invisible for the searches that matter

"Property maintenance Jacksonville," "handyman for property managers" — Google has almost nothing to rank. The managers searching right now aren't finding you.

→ Demand you never see
Issue 04

No proof for a B2B buyer

No service-area map, no list of the job types you handle, no testimonials from managers you already serve. Buyers need evidence before they trust you with a portfolio of properties.

→ Doubt where there should be confidence
Issue 05

Nothing catches the after-hours job

A pipe bursts at 9pm and the only option is voicemail. A request portal takes the job, the photos, and the address while you're still on another site.

→ Urgent work lost to whoever answers
The good news

None of this is about the work

It's all in the website and the intake flow — which means it's fast to fix and entirely in your control. The crew stays exactly as good as it is.
