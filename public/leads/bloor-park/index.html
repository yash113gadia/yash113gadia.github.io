The situation

A reputation your site isn't cashing in.

Bloor Park is, by the numbers, one of the most loved physiotherapy clinics in Toronto — a perfect 5.0 across 428 reviews. That kind of trust is rare and it's hard-won.

But your website tells a different story. It's a single, dated page last touched in 2018. So when someone in Koreatown searches "physiotherapy near me" or "pelvic floor physio Toronto," Google has almost nothing to show them — and the few who do land on the site see something that looks far less impressive than the clinic actually is. The gap between your reputation and your website is quietly costing you new patients every week.

First, what's already working

I'm not here to fix the clinic. I'm here to fix the front door.

✓
A genuinely exceptional reputation.

428 reviews at a flawless 5.0 — patients consistently name your therapists and come back. This is your single biggest asset, and it's barely visible online.

✓
A strong, specialised team.

Physiotherapy, pelvic health, massage, acupuncture, shockwave — real depth across services people actively search for.

✓
An established, trusted brand since 2018.

The foundation is solid. The redesign isn't a rebuild of who you are — it's an upgrade to how you're seen.

What's holding you back

Five fixable problems — each one costing you bookings.

i.

It's a single page, so Google can't rank you

Every service is crammed onto one page with anchor links — no dedicated pages for Physiotherapy, Pelvic Health, Massage, Shockwave, or the conditions people search by name. Search engines have almost nothing to index.

→ Invisible for high-intent local searches
ii.

The design doesn't match the reputation

An eight-year-old theme, a 2018 copyright, and small, dated photos. A first-time visitor judges the clinic by the site — and right now it reads older and smaller than you are.

→ Trust lost before the first call
iii.

428 five-star reviews are buried

Your strongest proof sits at the very bottom of the page. It should be the first thing a nervous, in-pain visitor sees.

→ Your best sales tool, hidden
iv.

Visible content slips hurt credibility

A raw code snippet printing on the page, a mislabelled local park, and small typos. Tiny things — but visitors read carelessness as "are they careful with me?"

→ Doubt where there should be confidence
v.

One generic button does all the converting

Every call-to-action is the same "Book Now." No service-specific paths, the free discovery call is hidden, and there's nothing for visitors who aren't ready to book yet. Likely weak on mobile, where most local searches happen.

→ Interested visitors leave without acting
→
