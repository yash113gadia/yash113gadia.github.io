The situation

A premium practice in a premium ZIP code.

Scottsdale patients have options, and they're discerning. Before they ever call, they judge a practice by its website — the photos, the polish, whether they can book without picking up the phone.

Yours is a text-heavy Weebly page that looks years behind the calibre of the dentistry inside. Google has little to index, mobile visitors strain to read it, and there's no way to book a visit in the moment they decide to. The practice is excellent; the website quietly says otherwise.

First, what's already working

I'm not fixing the dentistry. I'm fixing the first impression.

✓
Three credentialed dentists, real depth.

Dr. McAdams, Dr. Gibbons and Dr. Smith give you a breadth most single-dentist offices can't match — a genuine selling point the site barely mentions.

✓
A prime Scottsdale Road address.

High-visibility, high-income location. The web presence should look as established as the street you're on.

✓
An established, trusted name.

Years of reputation and citations across Yelp, Healthgrades and more. The redesign elevates how you're seen — it doesn't reinvent who you are.

What's holding you back

Five fixable problems — each one costing you new patients.

i.

A dated Weebly site undersells you

An old template and small photos read "behind the times" to patients comparing practices. In a premium market, that perception alone loses the appointment.

→ Trust lost before the first call
ii.

No way to book online

Patients decide at 10pm and want to lock a slot now. With phone-only booking, that intent evaporates by morning.

→ Ready-to-book patients slip away
iii.

Nearly invisible on Google

A single thin page gives search engines almost nothing for "dentist Scottsdale" or "cosmetic dentist Scottsdale." You're absent right where patients look.

→ Demand you never see
iv.

Three dentists, no real bios

Your breadth is a strength, but there are no proper profiles, photos or specialties — so a key reason to choose you stays hidden.

→ Your edge, unspoken
v.

Weak on the phone that matters most

Most local searches happen on mobile, where a dated template is hardest to read and act on. That's exactly where you're losing people.

→ Lost where patients actually look
→
